# Antipsychotic Hyperbolic Reduction: Receptor Theory vs Clinical Relapse Outcomes

> **Editorial status:** Specialist-review evidence draft. Do not publish until the named specialist and a clinical pharmacist have approved every medical and formulation claim. Evidence cutoff: **2026-08-18**. This page is educational and does not provide an individualized dose schedule.

## Key answer

Dopamine D2/D3 occupancy provides a plausible rationale for proportionally smaller antipsychotic reductions at low doses, but randomized evidence shows that reduction can increase relapse and has not proven functional benefit. The practical interpretation must remain within the population, duration, formulation, and outcomes that were actually studied. [1–2]

A safe interpretation starts with the exact medication or intervention, the reason it is being used, the formulation, treatment duration, other medicines, current symptoms, and the risks of both continuing and changing treatment. The article therefore explains decision factors and safety boundaries rather than prescribing a fixed schedule.

## The finding in one sentence

Dopamine D2/D3 occupancy provides a plausible rationale for proportionally smaller antipsychotic reductions at low doses, but randomized evidence shows that reduction can increase relapse and has not proven functional benefit.

## Why this question matters

Mechanistic curves are persuasive and can improve dose thinking, yet people with psychosis face high-consequence relapse. An evidence page must hold receptor theory and clinical outcomes together rather than choose one.

## What evidence was reviewed

Evidence includes imaging dose–occupancy studies, pharmacologic modeling, narrative proposals, observational experience, and randomized reduction trials such as RADAR. These sources answer different questions.

## What the studies found

Occupancy is nonlinear, so equal milligram cuts can produce larger target changes at low doses. This supports smaller proportional steps conceptually. RADAR found more relapse and no social-function advantage for the tested gradual reduction strategy at two years, demonstrating that plausible taper mechanics do not guarantee clinical success.

## Key limitations and sources of uncertainty

Occupancy varies by drug, dose, timing, metabolites, and individual biology. Relapse is influenced by illness, withdrawal, sleep, stress, and substance use. RADAR tested one population and strategy; mechanistic proposals lack strong comparative outcome trials.

## What this may change in practice

When reduction is considered, use specialist shared decisions, relapse prevention, small feasible dose options, adequate observation, and rapid access to care. Present hyperbolic reduction as a hypothesis-informed approach, not proven relapse prevention.

## What the evidence does not justify

Evidence does not justify universal discontinuation, promising that slow reduction prevents relapse, or dismissing a relapse as “only withdrawal” without urgent clinical assessment.

## What research should do next

Randomized trials should compare reduction shapes and depths, measure withdrawal and relapse, include first-episode and long-term groups, and assess function, cognition, adverse effects, and patient goals over years.

## Funding and conflict-of-interest note

Drug-development studies are often sponsored by manufacturers, while guideline panels and independent reviews can also include authors with industry relationships. Publication should list the funding source and declared conflicts for every pivotal study. A declared conflict does not invalidate a result, but it raises the importance of protocol registration, complete outcome reporting, independent replication, and separation of evidence from promotional language.

- A network meta-analysis is presented as a head-to-head randomized comparison

- Were withdrawal, relapse, function, serious adverse events, and long-term outcomes measured directly?
- What was not studied, and what uncertainty remains for my situation?
- Would this finding actually change a clinical decision, or only support further research?

## Frequently asked questions

### Does lower D2 occupancy mean lower relapse risk?

Not directly. Occupancy is one mechanism, while relapse is a complex clinical outcome.

### Did RADAR use hyperbolic tapering?

It used a supported gradual reduction strategy, but debates about exact taper shape do not replace the randomized findings.

### Can adverse effects improve at lower doses?

They may, but expected benefit must be balanced against relapse risk.

### Should an app display receptor curves?

Only as educational models with assumptions and a clear statement that they are not schedules.

## Regional and formulation note

Approval status, labels, and clinical practice vary by country. Verify the exact product label, manufacturer, strength, release system, and local clinical pathway before publication or use. A tablet or capsule instruction that is correct for one product may be wrong for another.
