# Benzodiazepine Dose Equivalence: Why Conversion Tables Are Only Estimates

> **Editorial status:** Specialist-review evidence draft. Do not publish until the named specialist and a clinical pharmacist have approved every medical and formulation claim. Evidence cutoff: **2026-08-18**. This page is educational and does not provide an individualized dose schedule.

## Key answer

How benzodiazepine equivalence tables are constructed, why values differ, and why a calculated diazepam equivalent is not a personal conversion dose. The central safety principle is: An approximate clinical comparison, often expressed as a diazepam-equivalent range. [1–3]

A safe interpretation starts with the exact medication or intervention, the reason it is being used, the formulation, treatment duration, other medicines, current symptoms, and the risks of both continuing and changing treatment. The article therefore explains decision factors and safety boundaries rather than prescribing a fixed schedule.

## Working definitions

| Term | Meaning in this article |
|---|---|
| Withdrawal symptoms | New or intensified symptoms after a dose reduction, missed dose, formulation change, or stop when physiologic adaptation is a plausible contributor. |
| Rebound | Temporary return of a treated symptom above its pretreatment baseline. |
| Relapse or recurrence | Return of the underlying treated condition. |
| Physical dependence | Physiologic adaptation; it is not the same as addiction or a substance use disorder. |

## There is no single biologic exchange rate

Benzodiazepines differ in receptor affinity, speed of absorption, half-life, active metabolites, lipid solubility, dosing frequency, and clinical indication. An equivalent for anxiety may not feel equivalent for sedation, seizure control, muscle relaxation, or interdose symptoms.

## How tables are built

Tables draw on pharmacology, clinical trials, withdrawal experience, therapeutic dose ranges, and expert consensus. Some provide one number; others provide a range. Precision to a decimal place can create false confidence when the underlying estimate is broad.

## Why a calculated conversion can fail

A person may be over-sedated after a nominally equivalent long-acting dose or experience withdrawal because the conversion underestimates their response. Liver impairment, older age, smoking, enzyme inhibitors, opioids, and alcohol can change risk.

## How clinicians reduce conversion risk

Clinicians may use staged substitution, monitor sedation and withdrawal, adjust for organ function, and preserve the option to pause. The transition itself is a medication change and should be separated from later taper reductions when feasible.

## How to present equivalence on RxDown

Display ranges, source, version, and a prominent “not an exact conversion” warning. Do not let the app automatically prescribe a target dose. Any calculator should support clinician discussion and record uncertainty.

## When urgent medical assessment may be needed

        Seek urgent local medical assessment when any of the following applies. This list is intentionally conservative and is not a diagnostic checklist:

        - A calculated conversion produces profound sedation or uncontrolled withdrawal
- The person has significant liver disease, frailty, or respiratory-depressant co-use
- The table source and version are unknown
- A calculator output is treated as a prescription

        Emergency pathways and telephone numbers vary by country. A tracking application or educational article cannot rule out an emergency.

## Questions to take to the prescriber

- What is the current indication and treatment goal?
- Which alternative explanations for the symptoms need assessment?
- What is the exact medication, formulation, timing, and most recent change?
- Which outcome should be monitored before another change is considered?
- What are the urgent warning signs and the fastest route back to the clinical team?

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do two reputable tables disagree?

They may use different evidence and endpoints. The disagreement reflects uncertainty rather than one table necessarily being fraudulent.

### Is a diazepam-equivalent dose equally sedating?

Not necessarily. Sedation, anxiety control, and withdrawal coverage are not identical endpoints.

### Can I convert all at once?

That may be unsafe for some people. The strategy and monitoring should be individualized.

### Should RxDown show a calculator?

Only with ranges, assumptions, source dates, and a clinician-review workflow—not as an instruction engine.

## Regional and formulation note

Guidelines, labels, formulations, and care pathways vary by country. Verify the exact product label, manufacturer, strength, release system, and local clinical pathway before publication or use. A tablet or capsule instruction that is correct for one product may be wrong for another.
