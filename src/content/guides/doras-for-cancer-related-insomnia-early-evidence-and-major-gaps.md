# DORAs for Cancer-Related Insomnia: Early Evidence and Major Gaps

> **Editorial status:** Specialist-review evidence draft. Do not publish until the named specialist and a clinical pharmacist have approved every medical and formulation claim. Evidence cutoff: **2026-08-18**. This page is educational and does not provide an individualized dose schedule.

## Key answer

DORAs have insomnia evidence in general adult populations, but oncology-specific evidence is limited and does not establish routine superiority for cancer survivors. The practical interpretation must remain within the population, duration, formulation, and outcomes that were actually studied. [1–4]

A safe interpretation starts with the exact medication or intervention, the reason it is being used, the formulation, treatment duration, other medicines, current symptoms, and the risks of both continuing and changing treatment. The article therefore explains decision factors and safety boundaries rather than prescribing a fixed schedule.

## Evidence basis and uncertainty

This draft prioritizes regulator-hosted product information, current clinical guidance, systematic reviews, randomized trials, and carefully labeled observational or mechanistic evidence. The references below are used according to their design and limitations [1–4]. Absence of a detected signal is not rewritten as absence of risk, and a population average is not presented as an individual prediction.

| Reference | Evidence type | How it is used in this draft |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Small retrospective pilot study | Only 14 patients, most with hyperactive delirium; not evidence for routine chronic cancer-survivor insomnia treatment. |
| 2 | Regulator-hosted product labeling | Trial-based withdrawal and rebound assessments must not be rewritten as universal absence of risk. |
| 3 | Oncology clinical practice guideline | Recommends screening and first-line CBT-I-based care for chronic insomnia in patients with cancer and cancer survivors. |
| 4 | Network meta-analysis | Indirect comparisons are not equivalent to head-to-head trials and must be interpreted with dose, duration, and outcome differences. |

## Working definitions

| Term | Meaning in this article |
|---|---|
| Withdrawal symptoms | New or intensified symptoms after a dose reduction, missed dose, formulation change, or stop when physiologic adaptation is a plausible contributor. |
| Rebound | Temporary return of a treated symptom above its pretreatment baseline. |
| Relapse or recurrence | Return of the underlying treated condition. |
| Physical dependence | Physiologic adaptation; it is not the same as addiction or a substance use disorder. |

## What DORAs are

Dual orexin receptor antagonists (DORAs) reduce wake-promoting orexin signaling. Daridorexant, lemborexant, and suvorexant have product-specific approvals and labels in some countries. Their general insomnia trials should be interpreted by exact generic name, dose, duration, population, and outcome; the class name does not erase product differences.

## What oncology-specific evidence exists

A small retrospective pilot reported lemborexant use in 14 patients with cancer-related insomnia; most had hyperactive delirium. Such a sample can generate a safety or feasibility signal, but it cannot estimate comparative effectiveness, long-term harms, recurrence, or routine outcomes in cancer survivors. Delirium-related sleep disturbance is not interchangeable with chronic insomnia disorder.

## Why general insomnia trials do not answer the cancer question

Pivotal insomnia trials often exclude unstable medical illness, complex drug interactions, active delirium, or advanced cancer. Oncology patients may receive strong CYP3A inhibitors or inducers, opioids, corticosteroids, antiemetics, and other sedatives. Organ impairment, falls, cachexia, sleep apnea, and treatment schedules can alter benefit and harm.

## Withdrawal and rebound wording

Product trials may include discontinuation assessments and may not detect a clear withdrawal syndrome under studied conditions. That is not proof that withdrawal cannot occur in every oncology patient, after every duration, or with every combination. Use wording tied to the studied trial conditions rather than a categorical class-wide absence claim.

## Where DORAs might be considered

A clinician may consider a DORA when medication treatment is appropriate after diagnosis, interaction review, and discussion of alternatives. Cancer history alone neither selects nor excludes the class. CBT-I remains first-line for chronic insomnia, with medication decisions integrated into symptom and oncology care.

## Research priorities

Needed studies should enroll active-treatment and survivorship populations, stratify by cancer and regimen, distinguish insomnia from delirium, measure next-day function and falls, include respiratory and interaction outcomes, report discontinuation systematically, and compare with CBT-I-supported care and other treatments. Independent replication is important because pivotal drug programs are commonly manufacturer-sponsored.

## When urgent medical assessment may be needed

        Seek urgent local medical assessment when any of the following applies. This list is intentionally conservative and is not a diagnostic checklist:

        - A delirium presentation is being treated as ordinary chronic insomnia without medical assessment
- A strong interacting medicine, severe liver disease, respiratory disorder, or multiple sedatives are present without pharmacy review
- A benzodiazepine or Z-drug is being abruptly stopped to start a DORA
- A small uncontrolled study is being presented as proof of routine cancer-survivor benefit

        Emergency pathways and telephone numbers vary by country. A tracking application or educational article cannot rule out an emergency.

## Questions to take to the prescriber

- Does this evidence apply to my diagnosis, age, treatment duration, formulation, and other medications?
- Was the finding based on a randomized comparison, an indirect comparison, an observational study, or mechanistic modeling?
- Were withdrawal, relapse, function, serious adverse events, and long-term outcomes measured directly?
- What was not studied, and what uncertainty remains for my situation?
- Would this finding actually change a clinical decision, or only support further research?

## Frequently asked questions

### Has lemborexant been proven for cancer-survivor insomnia?

No. Oncology-specific evidence is limited, and the small delirium-heavy pilot cannot establish routine survivorship effectiveness.

### Do DORA trial findings settle withdrawal risk for everyone?

No. Trials may not detect a clear syndrome under studied conditions, but they cannot settle risk for every person or use pattern.

### Can a DORA replace a benzodiazepine immediately?

There is no universal substitution method, and overlapping sedation plus benzodiazepine withdrawal require clinician oversight.

### Does CBT-I still matter?

Yes. It remains first-line for chronic insomnia, including oncology guidance.

## Regional and formulation note

Guidelines, labels, formulations, and care pathways vary by country. Verify the exact product label, manufacturer, strength, release system, and local clinical pathway before publication or use. A tablet or capsule instruction that is correct for one product may be wrong for another.
